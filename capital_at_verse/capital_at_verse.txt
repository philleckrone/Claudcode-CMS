REPORT OF CAPITAL AT VERSE
==========================

PURPOSE
Flags a capital letter at the start of a verse when the previous verse ended with non-sentence-final punctuation (e.g., a comma, semicolon, colon, or a plain word). This often indicates a sentence has been split across verses without appropriate punctuation at the end of the first verse.

Capitalized terms that appear in the project's TermRenderings.xml (proper names, divine titles, etc.) are excluded from flagging (option IgnoreBiblicalTerms, on by default). So are words that the project's Wordlist marks as always capitalized (option IgnoreWordlistCapitals, on by default). Capitalized names from an optional Toolbox/SFM dictionary (.dic) file can be ignored too: set NamesDictionaryFile and NamesDictionaryMarkers (off by default). A rendering can be a multi-word term (e.g. Nueva Jerusalen); the check requires the whole term, word for word in order, to appear at the verse start -- matching only the first word of a multi-word term is not enough to suppress the warning. Alternative renderings separated by || in a TermRendering entry (e.g. Nueva Jerusalen||Jerusalen Nueva) are each treated as their own separate term.
Wildcard entries in TermRenderings are supported: a rendering stored as
Dios* will also suppress Dioses; *Dios will suppress raDios; *Dios*
suppresses both prefixed and suffixed forms. Wildcards may be applied to any word of a multi-word term, not just single-word terms.

Note: only a term's first word needs to start with a capital letter to be eligible (matching this check's own trigger condition); a capitalized word buried in the middle of a multi-word term is no longer separately whitelisted on its own -- the full term must match.

Words written with decomposed tone or accent marks (a base letter followed by a separate combining mark, as in some Africanist/IPA-based orthographies) are matched and compared as whole units, and a verse ending in such a word is correctly recognized as ending in a letter rather than in the mark itself.

HOW TO USE
1. Copy the .cms and .py files, ptx_goto.py and ParatextGoTo.exe to your My Paratext 9 Projects\cms folder.
2. In Paratext, run the check from Project menu > Tools > Custom Tools > Phil_new.
3. Review the results list; each flagged item shows the reference and a text snippet around the problem.
4. A CapitalAtVerseReport.html report also opens automatically in your browser, showing three words of context before and after each flagged verse number, with any footnote, cross-reference, or figure excluded from that context.
5. Click a reference in the report to show that verse in Paratext. The first time, the browser asks whether to open ParatextGoTo; allow it (you can tick "always allow"). Paratext follows only when the text window's scroll group follows other programs (the same setting used for FieldWorks or Logos). Clicks need ptx_goto.py and ParatextGoTo.exe in the cms folder; without it the references are plain text. Running the check registers the link type for the current Windows user only; no administrator rights are needed.

OPTIONS
  FinalPunctuation
    Default:  .!?
    Characters that mark the end of a sentence. If the previous verse ends with one of these characters, the next verse is not flagged even if it begins with a capital letter. Add language-specific closing quotation marks here if they can end a sentence in your translation.

  WordsToIgnore
    Default:  (empty)
    Comma-separated list of capitalized words that should never be flagged,for example:  Senor, Alfa, Espiritu
    Use this for proper nouns that are not already covered by TermRenderings.xml.
    Accented and non-ASCII characters are supported; entries are compared after NFC normalization.
    This is also the base-word list that PrefixesToIgnore/SuffixesToIgnore build on: a listed word with one of those prefixes or suffixes attached is also not flagged.

  IgnoreBiblicalTerms  (Ignore capitalized Biblical Terms)
    Default:  Yes
    Set to Yes to skip a capitalized verse-start word or phrase that is a rendering in the project's Biblical Terms (TermRenderings.xml), as described under PURPOSE.  Set to No to flag these words too.  With No, PrefixesToIgnore/SuffixesToIgnore build only on WordsToIgnore and the Wordlist.

  IgnoreWordlistCapitals  (Ignore capitalized words in the Wordlist)
    Default:  Yes
    Set to Yes to skip a verse-start word that the project's Wordlist marks as always written with a capital (a word with a specific case in SpellingStatus.xml, e.g. Galilaaya).  The word must match exactly.  A listed word with one of the PrefixesToIgnore/SuffixesToIgnore attached is also skipped.

  NamesDictionaryFile  (Names dictionary file (optional))
    Default:  (empty = off)
    Optional.  Full path of a Toolbox/SFM dictionary (.dic) file whose capitalized names should not be flagged, for example C:\Dictionaries\names.dic.  Leave empty to turn the feature off.  The names are treated like capitalized Biblical Terms renderings, so PrefixesToIgnore/SuffixesToIgnore also build on them.
    If the file or the marker is not usable, the check says so in the results list and runs without the dictionary.

  NamesDictionaryMarkers  (Names dictionary markers)
    Default:  lx
    Comma-separated list of marker names (without backslash) whose values are read from the names dictionary file, for example:  lx  or  lx, a.  Any markers can be typed; only letters, digits and underscores are accepted (up to 32 characters each, up to 20 markers).
    The first word of each value is used, without a leading + and without anything after a / or |  (so "\a Tayta / V_ | note" gives Tayta).  Only names that begin with a capital letter are used.
    Has no effect while NamesDictionaryFile is empty.

  NamesDictionaryAllowSuffixes  (Names dictionary: allow suffixes)
    Default:  No
    Set to Yes to also skip any capitalized verse-start word that begins with a name from the names dictionary, for languages where suffixes combine too freely to list in SuffixesToIgnore.  Set to No to skip only exact names.
    Has no effect while NamesDictionaryFile is empty.

  IgnoreCapAfterQuote
    Default:  No
    Set to Yes to suppress errors where a verse begins with a capitalized word that immediately follows an opening quotation mark (e.g., direct speech introduced after a comma or semicolon).  The characters treated as openers are configured by the QuoteOpeningChars option below.

  QuoteOpeningChars
    Default:  «"—'
    Characters treated as opening quotation marks when IgnoreCapAfterQuote is set to Yes.  Each character in this string is an opener.  Default covers left guillemet («), left double curly quote ("), em dash (—), and left single curly quote (').  Clear this field to fall back to Unicode Pi-category detection.

  IgnoreStyles
    Default:  qs
    Comma-separated list of USFM character style names (without backslash) whose enclosed text is excluded when detecting the last character of a verse.  Use this for paired character styles like \qs...\qs* (Selah text in Psalms) or \pn...\pn* (proper names) that do not end with sentence-final punctuation.
    Example entry:  qs, pn  (the default is qs; add others after it)

  IgnoreParaStyles
    Default:  sp, r, ms, mr, sr, qa
    Comma-separated list of USFM paragraph marker names (without backslash) whose marker and following text are excluded when computing the last character of a verse.  Use this for structural markers that appear between verses but whose text should not count as the sentence-final character.
    Default markers covered:
      sp  - Speaker / Selah label (e.g., in Psalms or Song of Solomon)
      r   - Parallel passage reference
      ms  - Major section heading
      mr  - Major section range reference
      sr  - Section range reference
      qa  - Acrostic letter name (e.g., Aleph in Psalm 119)
    Note: Section headings (\s, \s1, \s2, etc.) are handled automatically by the check and do not need to be listed here.
    A paragraph break followed only by text in these styles also stops the check.  For example, when a verse is followed by \s1 heading, then \sp speaker, then \q1, the next verse is not checked (common in Song of Songs).
    To disable this feature entirely, clear the option value.

  PrefixesToIgnore
    Default:  (empty)
    Space-separated list of word prefixes (e.g.  ra  Ra).  A capitalized verse-start word made of one of these prefixes plus either a word listed in WordsToIgnore, or a capitalized Biblical Terms rendering this check already recognizes from TermRenderings.xml (case-insensitive), will not be flagged.  Has no effect on a word whose remainder after the prefix isn't recognized either way.
    Example entry:  ra ka
    An entry can also be a "/"-separated group of interchangeable variants, for languages where attaching the prefix changes the following letter(s) rather than just adding characters in front. If the word starts with any one variant in the group, each other variant is tried in its place when reconstructing the word to check against WordsToIgnore/TermRenderings.xml.
    Example (3-way vowel harmony):  i/ɨ/ʉ

  SuffixesToIgnore
    Default:  (empty)
    Space-separated list of word suffixes (e.g.  es  am).  A capitalized verse-start word made of one of these suffixes plus either a word listed in WordsToIgnore, or a capitalized Biblical Terms rendering this check already recognizes from TermRenderings.xml (case-insensitive), will not be flagged.  Has no effect on a word whose remainder before the suffix isn't recognized either way.
    Example entry:  es am pa
    An entry can also be a "/"-separated group of interchangeable variants, for languages where the stem's ending itself changes when a suffix attaches rather than the suffix simply being added on. If the word ends in any one variant in the group, each other variant is tried in its place when reconstructing the word to check against WordsToIgnore/TermRenderings.xml.
    Example (Spanish singular/plural):  ión/iones     (e.g. a word ending in Salvación is also recognized as ending in Salvaciones, and vice versa)
    Example (3-way vowel harmony):  i/ɨ/ʉ

  MidWordPunctuation
    Default:  -
    Characters that may appear inside a word and should not split it into separate tokens when checking for capitals.  Enter without spaces.
    The hyphen (-) is included by default to handle compound proper nouns such as Be-nimura or Ebel-setin.  To also allow apostrophes (e.g. k'am), add ' to the value:  -'
    To disable mid-word joining entirely, clear the option value.

  SkipBeforeVerse
    Default:  [
    Characters (no spaces) that may stand just before a verse number, such as the [ that opens a bracketed verse (verses not found in the oldest manuscripts).  They are looked past when finding how the previous verse ended:
      word. [ \v 11 Capital     - the previous verse counts as ending with . so verse 11 is not flagged
      \p [ \v 53 Capital       - the \p counts as a paragraph break, so verse 53 is not flagged
      word, [ \v 11 Capital     - still flagged, because the verse before the [ ends with a comma
    To treat these characters as ordinary characters, clear the option value.
